# Congestion ↔ topology-placement correlation (incast aggressor)

**Date:** 2026-07-02 · **Status:** preliminary (n64/256 KB & n64/2 MB at 15–16
reps; other cells 6–7)
**Data:** `data/leonardo/_sweep_analysis/repro_paper/congestion_agtr_inc_rep_{stats,detail}.csv`

## Setup

Paper-repro congestion cells (Leonardo Booster, HDR IB Dragonfly+). Victim =
ring AllGather (`agtr_comm_only`), aggressor = Incast (`inc_nb`, endless), 50:50
**interleaved** partition, aggressor leads by 10 s. Metric per run is the ratio
`uncongested/congested` victim runtime (**<1 = victim slowed**). Each cell was
run as several **reps** — separate Slurm allocations, i.e. different node
placements — to separate measurement noise from placement variance. Throughout,
**cv = coefficient of variation = std/mean** (relative spread across reps).

## 1. The effect is real but strongly placement-dependent

Averaged over reps, incast clearly slows the victim, growing with node count and
message size. The run-to-run spread is large — for n64 it is **bimodal**: some
reps are crushed (−60 %) while siblings escape (ratio ≈ 1.0).

| cell | ratio (mean ± std) | cv | min–max | reps |
|------|--------------------|----|---------|------|
| n32 / 256 KB | 0.985 ± 0.012 | 1% | 0.96–1.00 | 6 |
| n32 / 2 MB | 0.602 ± 0.068 | 11% | 0.57–0.74 | 6 |
| n32 / 16 MB | 0.713 ± 0.138 | 19% | 0.63–1.01 | 7 |
| n64 / 256 KB | 0.708 ± 0.183 | 26% | 0.48–1.01 | 16 |
| n64 / 2 MB | 0.462 ± 0.161 | 35% | 0.36–1.01 | 15 |
| n64 / 16 MB | 0.390 ± 0.074 | 19% | 0.31–0.53 | 6 |

A single run per cell would be misleading — the point estimate depends heavily on
which nodes Slurm handed out. The reps are genuinely independent draws (global
node intersection only 11–22 %, vs 88 % for back-to-back reps that reused nodes).

## 2. Locality metric

For each rep we classify every victim×aggressor host pair by topology distance
(from `topologies/leonardo.json`: node → cell, node → leaf switch):
`same_switch` / `same_cell` / `cross_cell`, plus the **cell span** of the whole
allocation. **`same_switch` = 0 in every rep** — at 50:50 interleaved these
allocations never place a victim and an aggressor node on the same leaf, so any
contention is decided at the **cell / spine** level.

## 3. Finding: coarse cell-locality does **not** explain the variance

At a fixed node count the topology metric barely moves: Slurm almost always
returns the **same span** — n64 ≈ 4 cells (15/16 reps), n32 ≈ 2 cells — with only
a rare rep one cell wider. Yet the ratio varies over nearly the full range
*within* that single span:

| cell | dominant span | ratio in that span bucket |
|------|---------------|----------------------------|
| n64 / 256 KB | span 4 (15 reps) | **0.48 – 1.01** (whole effect range) |
| n64 / 2 MB | span 4 (12 reps) | 0.36 – 0.51 |
| n64 / 16 MB | span 4 (4 reps) | 0.37 – 0.38 |
| n32 / 2 MB | span 2 (5 reps) | 0.57 – 0.59 |

Because the cross-cell fraction is likewise near-constant across a cell's reps
(n64 ≈ 0.74–0.77), the per-cell `corr(ratio, cross-cell)` is dominated by the 1–3
rare off-span reps and is **not robust**: with 6 reps it looked strong and
positive (e.g. n64/2 MB = +0.66), but at 15–16 reps it weakens and **splits in
sign** — n64/256 KB → **−0.49**, n64/2 MB → **+0.50**. The n32 cells stay positive
(n32/2 MB = +0.995) but that too rests on a single span-3 rep.

**Conclusion:** the congestion is real and highly placement-sensitive, but the
sensitivity is **finer-grained than the cell / span level** — the full 0.48→1.01
swing happens among reps that are identical in coarse locality. Cell-span and
cross-cell fraction do not predict which reps get congested.

## 4. Interpretation

Incast concentrates traffic onto a single destination's leaf/spine (a hotspot).
Whether the victim's ring is hit appears to depend on *which specific spine links
or root leaf* it shares with that hotspot — not on how many cells the job spans.
Two allocations with the same 4-cell span can put the incast root on a leaf the
victim heavily traverses (crushed) or off to the side (escape). Counting cells is
too coarse to see that; it needs **link-level path overlap**.

## 5. Next steps

- Replace the cell-span axis with a **fabric-level metric**: attribute the incast
  root's flows and the victim ring's flows onto shortest-path spine links (the
  existing `--fabric` / `--blame` machinery) and correlate the victim's ratio with
  the *shared-link load* between the two — the hypothesis coarse locality can't
  test.
- Extend to **n128/n256**, where the span axis has real range (and where the
  paper's effect is largest), to check whether cell-level locality gains
  explanatory power at scale.
- Report the two n64 modes separately (crushed vs escape) once enough reps pin the
  bimodal split.
